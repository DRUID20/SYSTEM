## 1. PURPOSE

To establish standardized procedures for conducting internal audit fieldwork, including planning individual engagements, gathering evidence, performing tests, documenting findings, and maintaining working papers. This SOP ensures consistency, quality, and completeness in all audit engagements.

---

## 2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to:
- All scheduled internal audit engagements under the annual audit plan
- All ad-hoc and special investigation engagements
- All members of the Internal Audit team performing fieldwork
- All departments and staff subject to audit

## 5. STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW

### CONTROLLED VERSION

#### Phase 1: Engagement Planning (Days 1-2)

**Step 1.1: Issue Engagement Letter**

The Head of IA issues a formal engagement letter to the auditee department head at least 5 business days before fieldwork begins. The letter includes:
- Audit objective and scope
- Estimated start and end dates
- Lead auditor and team members
- Key documents and access required
- Entrance conference date and time
- Request for auditee liaison/contact person

**Step 1.2: Review Background Information**

The lead auditor reviews:
- Prior audit reports for the area (last 2-3 engagements)
- Open CAPA items related to the area
- Relevant policies, SOPs, and process documentation
- Organizational structure and key personnel
- Recent changes (systems, personnel, processes)
- Financial data and transaction volumes

**Step 1.3: Develop/Update Detailed Audit Program**

Using the standard audit program for the area as a base, the lead auditor customizes:
- Specific audit objectives for this engagement
- Testing procedures and techniques
- Sample sizes based on population and risk
- Data requirements and sources
- Timeline for each audit section

**Step 1.4: Prepare Audit Tools**

- Prepare audit checklists (from templates)
- Set up working paper files (electronic or physical)
- Prepare data extraction requests
- Prepare interview schedules

#### Phase 2: Entrance Conference (Day 2 or 3)

**Step 2.1: Conduct Entrance Conference**

Attendees: Lead Auditor, Head of IA (if available), Auditee Department Head, Key auditee staff

Agenda:
1. Introductions and role of internal audit (independence, objectivity)
2. Audit objective, scope, and approach
3. Timeline and key milestones
4. Information and access requirements
5. Communication protocol during the audit
6. Identify auditee liaison/contact person
7. Address any auditee questions or concerns

**Step 2.2: Document Entrance Conference**

Record minutes including attendees, key discussion points, and agreed actions.

#### Phase 3: Preliminary Survey (Days 3-4)

**Step 3.1: Process Walkthrough**

- Walk through key processes with the auditee to understand actual practices
- Compare actual practices to documented procedures
- Identify control points and potential control gaps
- Document process flowcharts or narratives

**Step 3.2: Identify Key Controls**

For each process within scope, identify:
- Preventive controls (those that prevent errors/fraud)
- Detective controls (those that detect errors/fraud after occurrence)
- Manual vs. automated controls
- Key segregation of duties requirements

**Step 3.3: Refine Audit Program**

Based on the preliminary survey:
- Adjust testing focus areas
- Modify sample sizes if needed
- Add or remove test procedures
- Update timeline if necessary

#### Phase 4: Fieldwork and Testing (Days 5-15, varies by engagement)

**Step 4.1: Control Testing**

For each key control identified:

| Test Type | Description | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| Inquiry | Ask process owners about the control | All controls (supplementary) |
| Observation | Watch the control being performed | Real-time controls |
| Inspection | Examine documents and records for evidence | Document-based controls |
| Re-performance | Re-do the control to verify accuracy | Calculation-based controls |
| Data analytics | Analyze full data populations for anomalies | High-volume transactions |

**Step 4.2: Substantive Testing**

Test transactions for accuracy, completeness, authorization, and validity:

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| Vouching | Select recorded items and trace to supporting documents (tests overstatement) |
| Tracing | Select source documents and trace to records (tests completeness/understatement) |
| Recalculation | Independently recalculate amounts |
| Confirmation | Obtain third-party confirmation (bank, suppliers, customers) |
| Analytical procedures | Compare data, ratios, trends to expectations |
| Physical inspection | Verify physical existence (cash, inventory, assets) |

**Step 4.3: Sampling Methodology**

| Factor | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Population size <50 | Test 100% or minimum 25 items |
| Population 50-200 | Test minimum 25 items (random selection) |
| Population 200-500 | Test minimum 30 items (random or systematic) |
| Population >500 | Test 30-60 items (statistical or judgmental sampling) |
| High-risk items | Test all items above materiality threshold plus sample of remaining |
| Red flag items | Test 100% of flagged items |

**Step 4.4: Evidence Standards**

All audit evidence must be:
- **Sufficient** — Enough evidence to support the conclusion
- **Relevant** — Directly related to the audit objective
- **Reliable** — From credible, verifiable sources
- **Competent** — Appropriate quality for the conclusion drawn

Evidence types (in order of reliability):
1. Physical evidence (observation, inspection, count)
2. External confirmation (third-party documents)
3. Documentary evidence (internal documents)
4. Mathematical evidence (recalculation, data analytics)
5. Oral evidence (interviews, inquiries)

**Step 4.5: Document Working Papers**

For each test procedure, document:
- Objective of the test
- Population and sample selection method
- Test procedure performed
- Results (number tested, exceptions found)
- Conclusion (satisfactory / exception noted / finding)
- Supporting documents and references

Working paper standards:
- Each page has audit reference number
- Each page is dated and initialed by preparer
- Cross-references to audit program steps
- Clear, concise, and understandable by a reviewer
- Evidence attached or clearly referenced

#### Phase 5: Finding Development (Days 14-16)

**Step 5.1: Identify Exceptions and Issues**

From test results, identify:
- Control failures (control not operating as designed)
- Errors (transactions processed incorrectly)
- Policy violations (non-compliance with procedures)
- Inefficiencies (wasteful or redundant processes)
- Fraud indicators (intentional manipulation)

**Step 5.2: Develop Findings Using the "5 Cs" Format**

| Element | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Condition** | What was found (the current state) | "15 of 30 purchase orders tested lacked competitive quotations" |
| **Criteria** | What should be (policy, standard, regulation) | "Procurement Policy Section 4.2 requires three quotations for purchases above UGX 2,000,000" |
| **Cause** | Why it happened (root cause) | "Procurement staff unaware of revised policy thresholds; no training provided after policy update" |
| **Consequence** | Impact or potential impact | "Risk of paying above-market prices; estimated potential overpayment of UGX 15,000,000 on sampled transactions" |
| **Corrective Action** | Recommended action | "Conduct procurement policy training for all staff; implement pre-approval checklist for purchases above threshold" |

**Step 5.3: Rate Finding Risk Level**

| Rating | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Material financial loss, significant regulatory breach, or fraud detected; requires immediate action |
| **High** | Significant control weakness with potential for material impact; requires action within 30 days |
| **Medium** | Moderate control weakness with limited impact; requires action within 60 days |
| **Low** | Minor issue or improvement opportunity; requires action within 90 days |

**Step 5.4: Discuss Findings with Auditee**

Before finalizing:
- Share draft findings with the auditee department head
- Verify factual accuracy of the condition
- Discuss root cause and obtain auditee perspective
- Agree on the finding or document disagreement
- Obtain management response and action plan

#### Phase 6: Exit Conference (Day 16 or 17)

**Step 6.1: Conduct Exit Conference**

Attendees: Head of IA, Lead Auditor, Auditee Department Head, Key auditee staff

Agenda:
1. Summary of audit scope and work performed
2. Presentation of findings (condition, criteria, cause, consequence)
3. Discussion of recommendations
4. Obtain management responses and agreed action plans
5. Agree on action owners and target dates
6. Discuss any areas of disagreement
7. Outline the reporting timeline
8. Thank auditee team for cooperation

**Step 6.2: Document Exit Conference**

Record minutes including all findings discussed, management responses, agreed actions, and any unresolved items.

#### Phase 7: Quality Review (Days 17-18)

**Step 7.1: Lead Auditor Self-Review**

- Ensure all audit program steps are completed
- Verify all findings are fully developed (5 Cs)
- Confirm all working papers are complete and referenced
- Review evidence sufficiency and relevance

**Step 7.2: Head of IA Supervisory Review**

The Head of IA reviews:
- Audit program completion (all steps performed)
- Working paper quality and documentation standards
- Finding development and risk rating appropriateness
- Evidence sufficiency and reliability
- Logical consistency of conclusions
- Management responses and action plans adequacy

**Step 7.3: Address Review Notes**

Lead auditor addresses all review notes raised by Head of IA. Review notes and resolutions are documented.

### LEAN VERSION

For organizations with limited audit resources:

| Phase | Simplified Approach | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Brief notification email to auditee (2 days notice); review prior findings; prepare checklist | 0.5 day |
| Entrance | Informal meeting with department head to explain scope | 30 minutes |
| Fieldwork | Use standard checklist; test sample of 10-15 items per area; focus on highest-risk controls | 2-4 days |
| Findings | Document exceptions using simple format (What found / What expected / Impact / Recommendation) | 0.5 day |
| Exit | Brief meeting to discuss findings and obtain verbal agreement on actions | 30 minutes |
| Quality | Self-review using completion checklist | 0.5 day |

**Lean Evidence Standards:**
- Minimum of 10 items per test area
- Photographic evidence where appropriate (e.g., physical counts)
- Copies of key exception documents
- Signed checklist as primary working paper
