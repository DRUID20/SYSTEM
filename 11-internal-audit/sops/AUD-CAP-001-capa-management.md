## 1. PURPOSE

To establish a standardized system for tracking, managing, and closing audit findings through Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA). This SOP ensures that identified control weaknesses are addressed systematically, root causes are eliminated, and similar issues are prevented from recurring.

---

## 2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to:
- All findings from internal audit engagements (scheduled and ad-hoc)
- All findings from spot checks and surprise audits that require corrective action
- All findings from external audits that are tracked by Internal Audit
- Observations from management, regulators, or other assurance providers
- All departments and staff assigned as action owners
- The full lifecycle from finding identification to verified closure

## 5. STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW

### CONTROLLED VERSION

#### Phase 1: Finding Registration

**Step 1.1: Log the Finding**

Within 1 business day of report distribution, the lead auditor enters each finding into the CAPA tracker (AUD-TPL-002) with the following fields:

| Field | Data to Enter |
|---|---|
| Finding ID | Auto-generated: FND-YYYY-NNN |
| Source | Audit engagement ID / Spot check / External audit |
| Date Identified | Date the finding was documented |
| Audit Area | Category (Cash, Procurement, Payroll, etc.) |
| Finding Title | Brief descriptive title |
| Condition | What was found (current state) |
| Criteria | What should be (standard/policy) |
| Cause | Root cause of the gap |
| Consequence | Actual or potential impact |
| Risk Rating | Critical / High / Medium / Low |
| Recommendation | Auditor's recommended action |

**Step 1.2: Validate Risk Rating**

Head of IA reviews and confirms the risk rating based on:

| Rating | Financial Impact | Operational Impact | Regulatory Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | >UGX 50,000,000 or >5% revenue | Complete process breakdown | Regulatory sanction imminent |
| **High** | UGX 10,000,000 - 50,000,000 | Significant disruption | Non-compliance with material regulation |
| **Medium** | UGX 2,000,000 - 10,000,000 | Moderate inefficiency | Minor non-compliance |
| **Low** | <UGX 2,000,000 | Minimal impact | Improvement opportunity |

#### Phase 2: Root Cause Analysis

**Step 2.1: Determine Root Cause Analysis Depth**

| Risk Rating | RCA Method | Performed By |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Full 5 Whys + Fishbone Diagram | Audit team + Action owner + Dept Head |
| High | 5 Whys analysis | Audit team + Action owner |
| Medium | Simplified cause analysis (Why x 3) | Action owner with audit guidance |
| Low | Direct cause statement | Action owner |

**Step 2.2: Conduct Root Cause Analysis**

**5 Whys Example:**

| Level | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Why 1 | Why were purchase orders issued without competitive quotations? | Staff did not follow the procurement policy |
| Why 2 | Why did staff not follow the procurement policy? | They were unaware of the revised thresholds |
| Why 3 | Why were they unaware of the revised thresholds? | No training was conducted after the policy update |
| Why 4 | Why was training not conducted? | No formal process to communicate policy changes |
| Why 5 | Why is there no process to communicate policy changes? | Document management system lacks a change notification mechanism |
| **Root Cause** | — | **No systematic process for communicating policy changes to affected staff** |

**Step 2.3: Fishbone Diagram (for Critical findings)**

Categorize potential causes under:
- **People:** Skills, training, awareness, staffing levels
- **Process:** Procedures, workflows, controls, segregation of duties
- **Policy:** Adequacy, currency, communication, enforcement
- **Technology:** Systems, automation, access controls, data integrity
- **Environment:** Physical conditions, organizational culture, management tone

**Step 2.4: Document Root Cause**

Enter the validated root cause in the CAPA tracker. The root cause must be specific and actionable — not generic statements like "human error" or "oversight."

#### Phase 3: Corrective and Preventive Action Planning

**Step 3.1: Develop Corrective Action (Fix the Immediate Issue)**

The action owner, with guidance from the audit team, develops a corrective action plan that:
- Addresses the specific finding (the condition)
- Remedies any damage or loss already incurred
- Restores the control to its intended state
- Is realistic and achievable within the target timeframe

**Step 3.2: Develop Preventive Action (Prevent Recurrence)**

For High and Critical findings, a preventive action is also required:
- Addresses the root cause (not just the symptom)
- Implements systemic changes (policy, process, system, training)
- Prevents the same issue from occurring again
- May apply to other areas with similar risk

**Step 3.3: Define Action Plan Components**

| Component | Required For | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Corrective Action | All findings | Immediate fix for the finding |
| Preventive Action | High and Critical | Systemic change to prevent recurrence |
| Action Owner | All findings | Named individual responsible |
| Support Resources | As needed | Additional staff, budget, IT support required |
| Due Date | All findings | Target completion date (per risk rating) |
| Milestones | High and Critical | Interim checkpoints for longer actions |

**Step 3.4: Set Due Dates**

| Risk Rating | Maximum Due Date from Report | Milestone Check |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | 7 calendar days | Daily progress update |
| High | 30 calendar days | Weekly progress update |
| Medium | 60 calendar days | Bi-weekly progress update |
| Low | 90 calendar days | Monthly progress update |

**Step 3.5: Obtain Approval**

- Action owner's department head approves the action plan
- Head of IA reviews the action plan for adequacy
- If the plan is deemed inadequate, Head of IA discusses with action owner and department head
- If disagreement persists, escalate to MD

#### Phase 4: Implementation Monitoring

**Step 4.1: Progress Tracking**

Action owners provide progress updates per the schedule above:
- **Critical:** Daily email/call to Head of IA
- **High:** Weekly written update
- **Medium:** Bi-weekly written update
- **Low:** Monthly written update

**Step 4.2: CAPA Status Updates**

The lead auditor updates the CAPA tracker with:

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Finding logged; action plan not yet started |
| **In Progress** | Corrective action implementation underway |
| **Pending Verification** | Action owner reports completion; awaiting audit verification |
| **Verified — Closed** | Audit has verified effective implementation |
| **Overdue** | Due date passed without completion |
| **Escalated** | Issue escalated per escalation matrix |
| **Rejected** | Verification found implementation inadequate; returned to In Progress |

**Step 4.3: Automated Reminders**

(Controlled Version with system support)
- 7 days before due date: Reminder to action owner
- On due date: Alert to action owner and department head
- 1 day after due date: Alert to Head of IA
- 7 days overdue: Escalation alert per matrix

#### Phase 5: Verification and Closure

**Step 5.1: Verification Request**

When the action owner reports completion:
1. Action owner submits a completion notice with supporting evidence
2. Evidence may include: revised policies, training records, system screenshots, updated procedures, new forms in use

**Step 5.2: Verification Testing**

The assigned auditor verifies closure by:
1. Reviewing the evidence provided by the action owner
2. Performing limited re-testing:
   - Re-inspect a small sample of transactions processed after the corrective action
   - Observe the revised process in operation
   - Verify new controls are functioning
3. Assessing whether both the corrective and preventive actions address the root cause
4. Documenting the verification results

**Step 5.3: Closure Decision**

| Decision | Action |
|---|---|
| **Fully Resolved** | Mark as "Verified — Closed" in tracker; record closure date and verifier name |
| **Partially Resolved** | Return to "In Progress" with new due date; document what remains outstanding |
| **Not Resolved** | Return to "In Progress" or "Escalated"; notify department head and Head of IA |

**Step 5.4: Closure Documentation**

For each closed finding:
- Date of closure verification
- Evidence reviewed
- Verification results
- Name of verifying auditor
- Conclusion statement

#### Phase 6: Escalation

**Step 6.1: Escalation Matrix**

| Condition | Escalated To | Communication Method | Expected Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical overdue >7 days | ARC Chairman | Phone + Email | Emergency ARC meeting or directive |
| High overdue >15 days | Managing Director | Formal memo | Written directive to action owner |
| Medium overdue >30 days | Department Head + Head of IA | Formal letter | Revised action plan within 5 days |
| Any finding overdue >90 days | ARC (quarterly report) | Report | ARC direction to management |
| Pattern: 3+ same finding | ARC + MD | Special report | Systemic review initiated |
| Management non-cooperation | ARC Chairman | Confidential memo | ARC intervention |

**Step 6.2: Escalation Documentation**

All escalations are documented including:
- Original finding and due date
- Number of days overdue
- Communication attempts made
- Response received (or lack thereof)
- Escalation notification details
- Resolution or further escalation

#### Phase 7: Reporting and Trend Analysis

**Step 7.1: Monthly CAPA Status Report**

Distributed to management team:
- Total open findings by risk rating
- Aging analysis (on time / overdue / average age)
- Findings closed during the month
- New findings added during the month
- Top 5 overdue items

**Step 7.2: Quarterly CAPA Report to ARC**

Comprehensive report including:
- Executive summary of CAPA status
- Detailed tracker of all open items
- Closure rate analysis (by rating, department, audit area)
- Aging analysis with trend graphs
- Escalations made during the quarter
- Repeat findings analysis
- Systemic issues identified
- Recommendations for management action

**Step 7.3: Annual CAPA Analysis**

Year-end analysis covering:
- Total findings by category, rating, and department
- Closure rates and average time to close
- Year-over-year trends
- Root cause patterns
- Most common control failures
- Departments with highest finding density
- Effectiveness of CAPA process itself
- Recommendations for process improvement
