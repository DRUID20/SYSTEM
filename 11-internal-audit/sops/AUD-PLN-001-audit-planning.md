## 1. PURPOSE

To establish a standardized process for developing, approving, communicating, and monitoring the annual internal audit plan for Awdeer Investments Limited. This SOP ensures that audit resources are directed toward the highest-risk areas and that the plan aligns with organizational objectives.

---

## 2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to:
- The annual audit planning cycle for all AIL business units and functions
- All members of the Internal Audit team
- The Audit & Risk Committee in its oversight role
- All department heads who provide input to the planning process

## 5. STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW

### CONTROLLED VERSION

#### Step 1: Initiate Planning Cycle (November — Week 1)

1.1. Head of IA issues a planning initiation memo to the audit team outlining:
- Planning timeline and milestones
- Team responsibilities
- Data and information required

1.2. Schedule risk assessment interviews with:
- Managing Director
- All Department Heads
- Finance Manager
- ARC Chairman (if available)

1.3. Request the following documents from relevant departments:
- Updated risk register
- Strategic plan and annual objectives
- Financial performance reports (YTD)
- Regulatory correspondence and compliance status

#### Step 2: Gather Risk Intelligence (November — Weeks 1-2)

2.1. Review prior year audit reports and identify:
- Open findings not yet resolved
- Areas with repeat findings
- Control weaknesses noted

2.2. Review CAPA tracker for:
- Patterns of recurring issues
- Areas with high finding density
- Overdue corrective actions

2.3. Conduct risk assessment interviews:
- Use standardized interview questionnaire
- Document key concerns, emerging risks, and priorities
- Note any changes in operations, systems, or personnel

2.4. Review external sources:
- Regulatory changes (URA, NSSF, URSB)
- Industry trends and emerging risks
- IIA risk alerts and guidance

2.5. Analyze financial data:
- Transaction volumes and values by area
- Budget variances and unusual trends
- Key financial ratios

#### Step 3: Update Audit Universe (November — Week 3)

3.1. Review the current audit universe for completeness:
- Add new processes, systems, or business units
- Remove discontinued operations
- Update descriptions and responsible persons

3.2. Assign each audit universe item to a category:
- Financial, Operational, Compliance, IT, Governance

3.3. Document the updated audit universe in the standard format

#### Step 4: Perform Risk Assessment and Scoring (November — Weeks 3-4)

4.1. Score each audit universe item across the seven risk dimensions:
- Financial Impact (weight 25%)
- Likelihood of Occurrence (weight 20%)
- Complexity of Operations (weight 15%)
- Regulatory/Compliance Exposure (weight 15%)
- Prior Audit Findings (weight 10%)
- Time Since Last Audit (weight 10%)
- Management Concern (weight 5%)

4.2. Calculate composite risk scores using the weighted formula

4.3. Rank audit areas by composite risk score

4.4. Classify each area into risk tiers:
- Critical (4.0-5.0): Audit every 6 months
- High (3.0-3.99): Audit annually
- Medium (2.0-2.99): Audit every 18 months
- Low (1.0-1.99): Audit every 2-3 years

4.5. Document the risk assessment in the Risk Scoring Matrix

#### Step 5: Draft Annual Audit Plan (December — Weeks 1-2)

5.1. Determine audit coverage:
- All Critical and High risk areas must be included
- Medium risk areas included based on cycle and resource availability
- Low risk areas included if resources permit or management requests

5.2. Schedule audits across quarters considering:
- Seasonal factors (e.g., inventory counts near year-end)
- Staff availability and leave plans
- External audit coordination
- Auditee operational calendars

5.3. Estimate resource requirements for each engagement:
- Number of audit days (planning, fieldwork, reporting)
- Lead auditor and support staff
- Specialist skills or external co-sourcing needs

5.4. Include in the plan:
- Spot check schedule (monthly rotating)
- Follow-up reviews on prior findings (quarterly)
- Reserve days for ad-hoc audits and investigations (minimum 10% of total days)
- Training and professional development

5.5. Prepare the draft plan document including:
- Executive summary and planning methodology
- Risk assessment summary
- Detailed audit schedule by quarter
- Resource allocation table
- Budget (if applicable)

#### Step 6: Review and Approve (December — Weeks 3-4 / January — Week 1)

6.1. Present draft plan to the Managing Director for input and alignment

6.2. Incorporate MD feedback and finalize the draft

6.3. Present the plan to the Audit & Risk Committee for approval:
- Walk through risk assessment methodology
- Explain prioritization decisions
- Highlight any areas of concern or resource constraints
- Discuss coordination with external auditors

6.4. ARC approves the plan (with or without amendments)

6.5. Document ARC approval in meeting minutes

#### Step 7: Communicate and Implement (January — Weeks 1-2)

7.1. Issue the approved plan to:
- All Internal Audit team members (full plan)
- Managing Director (full plan)
- Department Heads (relevant sections and schedule)

7.2. Brief each audit team member on their assignments

7.3. Begin execution per the quarterly schedule

#### Step 8: Monitor and Adjust (Ongoing — Quarterly)

8.1. Head of IA tracks plan progress monthly:
- Engagements completed vs. scheduled
- Days utilized vs. budgeted
- Issues impacting the schedule

8.2. Prepare quarterly progress report for ARC including:
- Plan completion status (% complete)
- Summary of completed audits and key findings
- Changes to the plan (deferrals, additions, cancellations)
- Resource utilization
- Outlook for next quarter

8.3. Propose plan adjustments to ARC if needed:
- New high-risk areas identified
- Resources diverted to investigations
- Significant changes in business operations

8.4. ARC approves plan changes; updated plan is re-communicated

### LEAN VERSION

For organizations with limited audit resources (1 auditor, part-time or full-time):

#### Simplified Planning Steps

| Step | Activity | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review prior year findings and CAPA tracker | November, Week 3 |
| 2 | Informal risk discussions with MD and key managers | November, Week 4 |
| 3 | Update audit universe (focus on top 6-8 areas) | December, Week 1 |
| 4 | Score risk areas using simplified 3-factor model (Impact, Likelihood, Time Since Audit) | December, Week 1 |
| 5 | Draft plan covering 4-6 audits + monthly spot checks | December, Week 2 |
| 6 | Present to MD (and ARC if constituted) for approval | December, Week 3 |
| 7 | Communicate schedule to department heads | January, Week 1 |
| 8 | Review progress semi-annually | June and December |

#### Simplified Risk Scoring (Lean)

| Factor | Weight | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Financial Impact | 40% | 1-5 |
| Likelihood | 35% | 1-5 |
| Time Since Last Audit | 25% | 1-5 |

## 6. CONTROLS AND APPROVALS

| Control Point | Control | Responsible |
|---|---|---|
| Risk assessment methodology | Consistent application of scoring criteria across all areas | Head of IA |
| Plan approval | ARC formally approves the annual plan before execution | ARC Chairman |
| Plan changes | Any significant changes require ARC approval | Head of IA / ARC |
| Resource allocation | Sufficient resources allocated to cover high-risk areas | Head of IA / MD |
| Independence | No audit area excluded due to management pressure | ARC oversight |
| Quarterly review | Plan progress reviewed with ARC each quarter | Head of IA |
