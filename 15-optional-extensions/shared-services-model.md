# Awdeer Investments Limited — Shared Services vs Decentralized Model

**Document ID:** EXT-SSM-001
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** 2026-02-20
**Owner:** Managing Director / Chief Operating Officer
**Classification:** Internal

---

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a decision framework for determining which functions within Awdeer Investments Limited should be centralized as shared services at the Head Office (Kampala) and which should be decentralized to individual business lines (Energy, Real Estate, Logistics, Retail, Services) or branch locations. It also defines the cost allocation methodology and intercompany billing framework for shared services.

---

## 2. Guiding Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Cost efficiency | Centralize where pooling resources reduces total cost per transaction |
| Consistency | Centralize where uniform standards, compliance, and controls are critical |
| Speed of response | Decentralize where local decision-making speed drives revenue or customer satisfaction |
| Scalability | Centralize where a function must scale across all business lines without duplicating effort |
| Accountability | Decentralize where business line managers must own the P&L and operational outcomes |

---

## 3. When to Use Shared Services (Centralized at HQ)

Centralize a function when the majority of these conditions are true:

- The function requires specialized expertise that is expensive to duplicate
- Regulatory compliance demands uniform standards across all entities
- Transaction volumes benefit from pooling and automation
- The function does not require real-time physical presence at a branch
- Errors in the function carry significant financial, legal, or reputational risk
- Economies of scale are achievable through a single team

### 3.1 Functions Recommended for Shared Services

| Function | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **Finance & Accounting** | Uniform chart of accounts, IFRS compliance, URA tax filings, consolidated reporting. Single finance team ensures consistent month-end close, bank reconciliations, and management accounts across all business lines. |
| **Human Resources** | Centralized payroll (PAYE, NSSF compliance), uniform HR policies, recruitment standards, employee records management. Prevents policy inconsistency across business lines. |
| **IT & Digital Operations** | Single ERP instance, unified access control, cybersecurity, backup management, license management. Cost-prohibitive to duplicate IT teams per business line. |
| **Procurement** | Consolidated vendor management, bulk purchasing leverage, uniform purchase order process, fraud prevention through centralized approval. |
| **Legal & Compliance** | Contract review, regulatory filings, compliance register, AML/KYC oversight. Requires legal expertise that cannot be distributed to branches. |
| **Internal Audit** | Independence requires separation from operations. Centralized audit plan covers all business lines on rotation. |
| **Treasury & Cash Management** | Centralized bank relationships, cash pooling, foreign exchange management, investment decisions. |

---

## 4. When to Decentralize (Business Line / Branch Level)

Decentralize a function when the majority of these conditions are true:

- The function requires daily physical presence or local market knowledge
- Speed of decision-making directly impacts revenue or customer experience
- The business line manager must own results end-to-end
- Local conditions vary significantly between branches or business lines
- The function generates branch-specific data needed for local P&L accountability

### 4.1 Functions Recommended for Decentralization

| Function | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **Sales & Business Development** | Local market knowledge, relationship management, pricing negotiations, customer visits. Each business line has distinct sales cycles and customer profiles. |
| **Operations / Service Delivery** | Daily execution of core business activities. Energy operations differ fundamentally from retail or logistics operations. |
| **Branch-level Inventory Management** | Physical stock counts, receiving, issuing, and shrinkage control must happen where the stock is located. |
| **Branch Cash Handling** | Daily cash-ups, banking, petty cash — must be managed at the point of transaction. |
| **Customer Service** | Front-line customer interaction requires local presence and immediate response authority. |
| **Fleet / Logistics Dispatch** | Route planning, driver management, and dispatch require real-time local coordination (Logistics business line). |

## 6. Hybrid Model — Centralized Oversight with Local Execution

Some functions benefit from a hybrid approach where policy, standards, and reporting are centralized but daily execution is local.

| Function | Centralized Component | Decentralized Component |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------|
| HR | Payroll processing, policy design, benefits admin, compliance | On-site HR coordination, local recruitment interviews, staff scheduling |
| Procurement | Vendor approval, contracts above UGX 10M, strategic sourcing | Local purchase requisitions, receiving goods, urgent small purchases below threshold |
| Inventory | Valuation method, system of record, quarterly full counts | Daily stock counts, physical receiving/issuing, shrinkage reporting |
| Cash Management | Bank relationships, cash flow forecasting, investment | Daily branch cash-ups, local banking deposits |
| IT | Infrastructure, ERP, security policy, licenses | First-line support (branch IT champion), local printer/network issues |

## 7. Cost Allocation Methodology for Shared Services

### 7.1 Allocation Basis Options

| Allocation Basis | Best Used For | Calculation |
|-----------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Headcount** | HR services, IT support, general admin | (Business line headcount / Total company headcount) x Shared service cost |
| **Revenue** | Finance, procurement, legal | (Business line revenue / Total company revenue) x Shared service cost |
| **Usage / Transaction Volume** | IT (licenses, tickets), procurement (PO count) | (Business line transactions / Total transactions) x Shared service cost |
| **Direct Assignment** | Costs clearly attributable to one business line | Full cost charged to that business line |
| **Equal Split** | Board-level costs, corporate governance | Total cost / Number of business lines |

### 7.2 Allocation Method by Shared Service

| Shared Service | Primary Allocation Basis | Secondary Basis | Review Frequency |
|---------------|-------------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Finance & Accounting | Revenue | Transaction volume | Quarterly |
| Treasury | Revenue | N/A | Quarterly |
| HR — Payroll | Headcount | N/A | Monthly (payroll cycle) |
| HR — Recruitment | Headcount | Actual hires per business line | Quarterly |
| IT — Infrastructure | Headcount | N/A | Quarterly |
| IT — Licenses | Usage (license count) | N/A | Monthly |
| IT — Support | Ticket volume | Headcount (fallback) | Quarterly |
| Procurement | PO count / PO value | Revenue (fallback) | Quarterly |
| Legal & Compliance | Revenue | Direct assignment for specific matters | Quarterly |
| Internal Audit | Revenue | Direct assignment for special investigations | Annually |
| Office / Admin (rent, utilities, security) | Headcount (floor space where available) | N/A | Quarterly |
| Board & Governance | Equal split | N/A | Annually |

### 7.3 Allocation Process

**Step 1 — Capture Costs**
All shared service costs are captured in the Corporate cost center (Business Line Code 01 — CORP) in the Chart of Accounts.

**Step 2 — Determine Drivers**
At each allocation period, extract the allocation driver data:
- Headcount from HR system (as at last day of the period)
- Revenue from management accounts (period revenue)
- Transaction volumes from ERP (PO count, IT tickets, etc.)

**Step 3 — Calculate Allocation**
Apply the formula:

```
Allocated Cost to BL(x) = Total Shared Service Cost x (BL(x) Driver / Total Driver)
```

**Example — Finance team cost allocation by revenue:**

| Business Line | Revenue (UGX M) | Revenue Share | Finance Team Cost (UGX M) | Allocated (UGX M) |
|--------------|-----------------|---------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Energy | 2,400 | 40% | 180 | 72.0 |
| Real Estate | 1,200 | 20% | 180 | 36.0 |
| Logistics | 1,200 | 20% | 180 | 36.0 |
| Retail | 600 | 10% | 180 | 18.0 |
| Services | 600 | 10% | 180 | 18.0 |
| **Total** | **6,000** | **100%** | **180** | **180.0** |

**Step 4 — Post Journal Entries**
Post allocation journals monthly or quarterly:
- Debit: Business Line operating expense (account 7xxx-BL-01)
- Credit: Corporate shared service cost center (account 7xxx-01-01)

**Step 5 — Report and Review**
Include shared service allocations as a line item in each business line P&L. Review allocation bases quarterly; adjust if a business line's share changes by more than 5 percentage points.

## 8. Intercompany Billing Framework

### 8.1 When Intercompany Billing Applies

Intercompany billing is used when:
- A shared service provides a specific, measurable service to a business line or branch
- One business line provides goods or services to another business line
- Transfer pricing documentation is required for tax compliance
- The company structure includes separate legal entities (subsidiaries)

### 8.2 Intercompany Billing Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Arm's length pricing | Intercompany charges must reflect what would be charged to an external party for the same service |
| Documentation | Every intercompany charge must be supported by a service agreement or cost-plus calculation |
| Consistency | Apply the same methodology each period; do not change mid-year without CFO approval |
| Elimination on consolidation | All intercompany charges net to zero in consolidated accounts |
| Tax compliance | Transfer pricing must comply with Uganda Revenue Authority guidelines and the Income Tax Act |

### 8.3 Pricing Methods

| Method | Description | Used For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Cost-plus** | Actual cost + agreed markup (typically 5-15%) | Shared services where no market price exists |
| **Market rate** | Charge at prevailing market price | Services that could be sourced externally (IT support, legal) |
| **Direct recharge** | Pass-through at actual cost, no markup | Specific expenses incurred on behalf of another business line (travel, licenses) |

### 8.4 Intercompany Service Agreement

Each shared service must maintain a service agreement with each business line it serves. The agreement includes:

1. **Service description** — What the shared service provides (e.g., "monthly management accounts, tax filings, bank reconciliation")
2. **Service levels** — Measurable commitments (e.g., "month-end close by BD 10, payroll processed by 25th of each month")
3. **Pricing basis** — Cost-plus %, market rate, or direct recharge
4. **Billing frequency** — Monthly or quarterly
5. **Dispute resolution** — Escalation to CFO, then MD
6. **Review date** — Annual review, aligned with budget cycle

### 8.5 Intercompany Invoice Process

```
Step 1: Shared service calculates charges per the service agreement
         |
Step 2: Shared service raises intercompany invoice (ERP intercompany module)
         |
Step 3: Business line manager reviews and approves (or disputes within 5 business days)
         |
Step 4: Finance posts the intercompany journal:
         - Debit: Business line expense (receiving entity)
         - Credit: Corporate revenue / cost recovery (providing entity)
         |
Step 5: On consolidation, Finance eliminates all intercompany balances
```

### 8.6 Intercompany Account Codes

Per the Chart of Accounts (FIN-SYS-001), intercompany transactions use Branch/Location Code **99**:

| Account | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| 1300-xx-99 | Intercompany receivable (from business line xx) |
| 2200-xx-99 | Intercompany payable (to business line xx) |
| 8100-xx-99 | Intercompany management fee income |
| 7500-xx-99 | Intercompany management fee expense |

### 8.7 Monthly Intercompany Reconciliation

The Finance team must reconcile all intercompany balances monthly as part of the month-end close:

1. Extract intercompany receivable and payable balances by business line
2. Confirm matching entries on both sides (receivable in one = payable in the other)
3. Investigate and resolve any mismatches before closing the period
4. Prepare an intercompany elimination schedule for consolidated reporting
5. File the reconciliation as supporting documentation for the month-end close pack
