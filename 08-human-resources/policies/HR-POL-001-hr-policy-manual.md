## 1. Recruitment Policy

### 1.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited is committed to attracting, selecting, and appointing the best-qualified candidates through a fair, transparent, and non-discriminatory recruitment process. All recruitment shall comply with the Employment Act, 2006 and the Equal Opportunities Commission Act, 2007.

### 1.2 Scope

This policy applies to all recruitment activities for permanent, contract, temporary, and intern positions across all departments.

### 1.3 Approval to Hire

No recruitment shall commence without a formally approved Staff Requisition Form.

| Grade Level | Requisition Raised By | Approved By (Lean) | Approved By (Controlled) |
|-------------|----------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| G1 — Junior | Line Manager | HR Manager | HR Manager → Department Head |
| G2 — Officer | Line Manager | HR Manager | HR Manager → Department Head |
| G3 — Manager | Department Head | Managing Director | HR Manager → CFO → Managing Director |
| G4 — Senior Manager | Department Head | Managing Director | HR Manager → CFO → Managing Director |
| G5 — Director | Managing Director | Board | HR Manager → MD → Board HR Committee |

### 1.4 Internal Recruitment

- All vacancies shall first be advertised internally for a minimum of **5 working days**
- Internal candidates who meet the minimum requirements must be considered before external advertising
- Internal transfers and promotions follow the same selection criteria as external hires
- Employees must have completed at least **12 months** in their current role to apply for internal moves (waivable by MD)

### 1.5 External Recruitment

External recruitment is initiated when:
- No suitable internal candidate is identified
- The role requires specialist skills not available internally
- The hiring manager and HR agree that external sourcing is necessary

External sourcing channels:
- Company website and social media
- Job boards (e.g., BrighterMonday Uganda, Fuzu)
- Recruitment agencies (for G4+ or specialised roles)
- University career offices (for entry-level/intern positions)
- Employee referral programme

### 1.6 Selection Process

| Step | Lean Version | Controlled Version |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1. Application screening | HR Officer screens CVs | HR Officer screens → HR Manager shortlists |
| 2. Initial assessment | — | Written test / skills assessment |
| 3. First interview | Line Manager + HR Officer | Panel: HR + Line Manager + cross-functional member |
| 4. Second interview | — (if needed, MD) | Department Head or MD (for G3+) |
| 5. Reference checks | HR Officer (2 references) | HR Officer (3 references; background check for G3+) |
| 6. Medical examination | For field/physical roles | For all roles (G3+ include credit check for finance) |
| 7. Offer | HR Manager prepares; MD approves (G3+) | HR Manager prepares → Department Head → MD approves |

### 1.7 Equal Opportunity

- No candidate shall be discriminated against based on gender, age, ethnicity, religion, disability, marital status, or HIV status
- Job advertisements shall use gender-neutral language
- Interview panels should include at least one member of each gender where possible
- Reasonable accommodation shall be provided for candidates with disabilities

### 1.8 Recruitment Timelines

| Activity | Target Duration |
|----------|----------------|
| Requisition approval | 3 working days |
| Internal posting | 5 working days |
| External advertising | 10–14 working days |
| Shortlisting | 5 working days from closing |
| Interviews | Within 10 working days of shortlisting |
| Offer issuance | Within 5 working days of final interview |
| Total time-to-hire target | 30–45 working days |

## 2. Onboarding Policy

### 2.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited provides a structured onboarding programme to ensure new employees are effectively integrated into the organisation, understand their roles, and have the tools and knowledge to succeed.

### 2.2 Scope

This policy applies to all new employees, including transfers, promotions, and re-hires.

### 2.3 Pre-Boarding (Before Day 1)

Before the new employee's start date, the following must be completed:

| Task | Responsible | Timeline |
|------|------------|----------|
| Send signed offer letter and contract | HR Officer | Within 3 days of acceptance |
| Request pre-employment documents (ID, TIN, NSSF, certificates) | HR Officer | With offer letter |
| Set up workstation, email, system access | IT + Admin | 2 days before start |
| Assign buddy/mentor (Controlled version) | HR Manager + Line Manager | 3 days before start |
| Prepare onboarding pack (policies, handbook, stationery) | HR Officer | 2 days before start |
| Notify team of new joiner | Line Manager | 3 days before start |

### 2.4 Day 1 — Orientation

| Activity | Duration | Responsible |
|----------|----------|------------|
| Welcome and introduction to team | 30 minutes | Line Manager |
| Office tour (facilities, safety exits, first aid) | 30 minutes | HR Officer / Admin |
| HR orientation (policies, code of conduct, benefits overview) | 1.5 hours | HR Officer |
| IT setup and systems training | 1 hour | IT Officer |
| Complete all onboarding paperwork | 1 hour | HR Officer + Employee |
| Lunch with team / buddy | — | Line Manager / Buddy |
| Review of first week plan | 30 minutes | Line Manager |

### 2.5 First Week (Days 1–5)

- Complete all mandatory document submissions
- Complete IT and systems training
- Review job description and performance expectations with line manager
- Begin role-specific training
- Meet key stakeholders and cross-functional contacts
- Read and acknowledge Code of Conduct and key policies

### 2.6 First Month (Days 1–30)

- Complete all compliance training (anti-harassment, data protection, HSE)
- Establish 90-day goals with line manager
- Begin contributing to core job responsibilities
- First informal check-in with line manager (end of week 2)
- Second informal check-in with HR (end of week 4)
- Controlled version: Buddy/mentor meets weekly; HR conducts structured 30-day review

### 2.7 First 90 Days (Probation Period)

- Formal probation review at 90 days (see Section 3)
- Monthly check-ins with line manager documented
- Training plan completion monitored
- Performance against 90-day goals assessed

### 2.8 Onboarding Success Measures

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Day 1 completion rate (all admin tasks) | 100% |
| 30-day check-in completion | 100% |
| New hire satisfaction score (30-day survey) | >80% |
| 90-day retention rate | >90% |
| Time to full productivity | Within 90 days |

## 3. Probation and Confirmation Policy

### 3.1 Policy Statement

All new employees serve a probationary period during which their suitability for the role is assessed. This policy ensures fair evaluation and timely confirmation or separation decisions.

### 3.2 Probation Period

**ASSUMPTION:** The standard probation period is **3 months (90 calendar days)** from the date of joining.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Duration | 3 months (standard) |
| Extension | Up to 3 additional months (once only) with written justification |
| Notice during probation | 2 weeks by either party (per Employment Act, 2006) |
| Benefits during probation | Full statutory benefits (NSSF, PAYE); medical insurance from Day 1; annual leave accrues but cannot be taken in first month |

### 3.3 Probation Assessment

| Assessment Point | When | Conducted By | Documentation |
|-----------------|------|-------------|--------------|
| Informal check-in 1 | End of Month 1 | Line Manager | Brief notes |
| Informal check-in 2 | End of Month 2 | Line Manager | Brief notes |
| Formal probation review | End of Month 3 | Line Manager + HR Manager | Probation Review Form |

### 3.4 Assessment Criteria

Employees are assessed on:

1. **Job competence** — Ability to perform core duties as described in the job description
2. **Quality of work** — Accuracy, thoroughness, and attention to detail
3. **Productivity** — Volume and timeliness of output
4. **Attitude and behaviour** — Professionalism, teamwork, initiative
5. **Attendance and punctuality** — Reliability and adherence to work schedule
6. **Alignment with values** — Demonstration of company values and code of conduct

Each criterion is rated: **Exceeds / Meets / Partially Meets / Does Not Meet**

### 3.5 Probation Outcomes

| Outcome | Criteria | Action |
|---------|----------|--------|
| **Confirmed** | Meets or exceeds all criteria | Issue confirmation letter; update employee status |
| **Extended** | Partially meets some criteria; improvement expected | Issue extension letter (max 3 months); set clear improvement targets |
| **Terminated** | Does not meet criteria; no reasonable prospect of improvement | Issue termination notice (2 weeks); process final pay |

### 3.6 Approval for Probation Decisions

| Decision | Lean Version | Controlled Version |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Confirm | Line Manager + HR Manager | Line Manager → HR Manager → Department Head |
| Extend | HR Manager + MD | Line Manager → HR Manager → MD |
| Terminate | HR Manager + MD | HR Manager → Legal review → MD |

## 4. Leave and Attendance Policy

### 4.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited provides leave entitlements that meet or exceed the minimum requirements of the Employment Act, 2006. The company expects regular attendance and punctuality from all employees.

### 4.2 Working Hours

| Parameter | Standard |
|-----------|----------|
| Working days | Monday to Friday |
| Working hours | 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM (with 1-hour lunch break) |
| Total weekly hours | 40 hours |
| Saturday work | As required by role (with time-off-in-lieu or overtime for eligible grades) |

### 4.3 Leave Entitlements

**ASSUMPTION:** Annual leave is set at 21 working days, above the statutory minimum of 7 days.

| Leave Type | Entitlement | Statutory Basis | Conditions |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|------------|
| **Annual leave** | 21 working days per year | Employment Act s.54 (minimum 7 days) | Accrues monthly (1.75 days/month); pro-rated in first year |
| **Sick leave** | 14 working days per year (full pay) + 14 days at half pay | Employment Act s.55 | Medical certificate required after 2 consecutive days |
| **Maternity leave** | 60 working days (full pay) | Employment Act s.56 | 4 weeks before and 8 weeks after delivery; medical certificate required |
| **Paternity leave** | 4 working days | Employment Act s.57 | Within 7 days of birth; birth notification required |
| **Compassionate leave** | 5 working days per year | Company policy | Death or serious illness of immediate family member |
| **Study leave** | Up to 10 working days per year | Company policy | Pre-approved course or examination; documentation required |
| **Unpaid leave** | Up to 30 days per year | Company policy | MD approval required; no salary or benefits during leave |
| **Public holidays** | As gazetted by Government of Uganda | Public Holidays Act | Typically 10–12 days per year |

### 4.4 Leave Rules

1. **Advance notice:** Annual leave requests must be submitted at least **14 days** in advance; managers may require 30 days for periods exceeding 5 consecutive days
2. **Leave planning:** Departments must prepare annual leave rosters by January 31st each year
3. **Carry-over:** Maximum **5 days** of unused annual leave may be carried to the next year; must be used by March 31st
4. **Encashment:** Leave encashment is not permitted except upon termination of employment
5. **Concurrent leave:** No more than **30%** of a department's staff may be on leave simultaneously
6. **Leave during probation:** Annual leave accrues during probation but may not be taken in the first month
7. **Sick leave documentation:** Medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner required for absences exceeding 2 consecutive days
8. **Maternity notification:** Employee must notify employer at least **4 weeks** before expected date of confinement

### 4.5 Attendance Management

| Attendance Issue | First Occurrence | Repeat Occurrence |
|-----------------|-----------------|------------------|
| Late arrival (1–15 minutes) | Verbal reminder by manager | Verbal warning (documented) |
| Late arrival (>15 minutes) | Verbal warning | Written warning |
| Absence without notification | Written warning | Final written warning |
| Absence without permission (AWOL) | Final written warning | Dismissal (after hearing) |
| Pattern of Monday/Friday absences | Investigation by HR | Disciplinary action |

### 4.6 Leave Approval Matrix

| Leave Type | Lean Approval | Controlled Approval |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Annual leave (1–5 days) | Line Manager | Line Manager |
| Annual leave (6+ days) | Line Manager + HR Manager | Line Manager → Department Head |
| Sick leave (1–2 days) | Line Manager (notification) | Line Manager (notification) |
| Sick leave (3+ days) | Line Manager + medical cert to HR | Line Manager + medical cert reviewed by HR |
| Maternity/Paternity | HR Manager | HR Manager → Department Head (for planning) |
| Compassionate | Line Manager + HR Manager | Line Manager → HR Manager |
| Study leave | HR Manager + MD | HR Manager → Department Head → MD |
| Unpaid leave | MD | HR Manager → Department Head → MD |

## 5. Disciplinary and Grievance Policy

### 5.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited maintains fair and consistent disciplinary procedures in compliance with the Employment Act, 2006 (Sections 65–75). Every employee has the right to be heard before any disciplinary action is taken. Equally, every employee has the right to raise grievances through a structured process.

### 5.2 Scope

This policy applies to all employees regardless of grade, tenure, or employment type.

### 5.3 Disciplinary Framework

#### 5.3.1 Progressive Discipline Steps

| Step | Action | Issued By | Valid For | Documented |
|------|--------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Step 1** | Verbal Warning | Line Manager | 3 months | Brief note in file |
| **Step 2** | First Written Warning | Line Manager + HR | 6 months | Formal warning letter |
| **Step 3** | Final Written Warning | HR Manager + Department Head | 12 months | Formal warning letter |
| **Step 4** | Dismissal | MD (after hearing) | Permanent | Termination letter |

*Note: Steps may be skipped in cases of gross misconduct (see Section 5.3.3).*

#### 5.3.2 Misconduct Categories

| Category | Examples | Starting Step |
|----------|---------|--------------|
| **Minor misconduct** | Lateness, dress code violation, minor negligence | Step 1 |
| **Moderate misconduct** | Repeated minor offences, insubordination, unauthorised absence | Step 2 |
| **Serious misconduct** | Breach of confidentiality, negligence causing loss, policy violation | Step 3 |
| **Gross misconduct** | Fraud, theft, violence, sexual harassment, intoxication at work, gross insubordination | Step 4 (dismissal) |

#### 5.3.3 Gross Misconduct — Summary Dismissal

Per Section 69 of the Employment Act, 2006, the following may warrant summary dismissal:

1. Theft, fraud, or dishonesty
2. Wilful damage to employer's property
3. Gross negligence or dereliction of duty
4. Violence or physical assault
5. Sexual harassment (ref: Employment (Sexual Harassment) Regulations, 2012)
6. Being under the influence of alcohol or drugs at work
7. Gross insubordination
8. Conviction of a criminal offence related to employment
9. Unauthorised disclosure of confidential information

Even in gross misconduct cases, the employee must be given an opportunity to be heard before dismissal.

### 5.4 Disciplinary Hearing Process

1. **Notice:** Employee receives written notice of the hearing at least **48 hours** in advance, specifying the charges
2. **Representation:** Employee may be accompanied by a colleague or union representative
3. **Hearing panel:**
   - Lean: HR Manager + Department Head
   - Controlled: HR Manager + Department Head + independent manager
4. **Procedure:**
   - Charges read and explained
   - Employee given opportunity to respond
   - Witnesses heard (if applicable)
   - Panel deliberates in private
   - Decision communicated in writing within **3 working days**
5. **Appeal:** Employee may appeal in writing to the MD within **7 working days** of the decision

### 5.5 Grievance Procedure

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| **Step 1** | Employee raises concern with immediate supervisor verbally | — |
| **Step 2** | If unresolved, employee submits written grievance to HR Manager | Within 7 days |
| **Step 3** | HR Manager investigates and responds in writing | Within 10 working days |
| **Step 4** | If unresolved, escalate to MD / Grievance Committee | Within 5 working days |
| **Step 5** | MD / Committee makes final decision | Within 10 working days |
| **Step 6** | If still unresolved, employee may refer to the Industrial Court | Per statutory timelines |

### 5.6 Documentation

All disciplinary and grievance records must be:
- Filed in the employee's personnel file
- Kept confidential
- Retained for the duration of employment plus 7 years
- Made available to the employee upon request (their own records)

- HR-DIS-001 Disciplinary and Grievance SOP
- HR-TPL-007 Disciplinary Notice Template

## 6. Performance Review Policy

### 6.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited operates a structured performance management system to align individual contributions with organisational goals, support employee development, and inform decisions on rewards, promotions, and separations.

### 6.2 Scope

All employees who have completed probation are subject to the performance review process.

### 6.3 Performance Cycle

| Activity | Timing | Duration |
|----------|--------|----------|
| Goal setting | January (start of year) | 2 weeks |
| Q1 check-in | End of March | 30-minute meeting |
| Q2 check-in (mid-year review) | End of June | 45-minute meeting |
| Q3 check-in | End of September | 30-minute meeting |
| Annual appraisal | November–December | 60-minute meeting + documentation |
| Calibration session (Controlled) | December | HR + Senior Management |

### 6.4 Quarterly Check-Ins

Quarterly check-ins are informal but documented conversations between the employee and line manager covering:

1. Progress against goals
2. Challenges and support needed
3. Development actions
4. Feedback (both directions)
5. Any goal adjustments

**Lean version:** Brief notes captured by manager; filed with HR quarterly.
**Controlled version:** Structured form completed and signed by both parties; entered in HRIS.

### 6.5 Annual Appraisal

The annual appraisal is a comprehensive review covering:

| Component | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Goal achievement | 50% | Assessment of KPIs and objectives set at start of year |
| Competency assessment | 30% | Role-specific and behavioural competencies |
| Values alignment | 10% | Demonstration of company values |
| Development progress | 10% | Completion of training and development actions |

### 6.6 Rating Scale

| Rating | Description | Indicative Salary Action |
|--------|-------------|------------------------|
| **5 — Outstanding** | Consistently exceeds all expectations; exceptional contribution | 8–10% increment; promotion consideration |
| **4 — Exceeds Expectations** | Regularly exceeds most expectations | 6–8% increment |
| **3 — Meets Expectations** | Fully meets all role requirements | 4–6% increment |
| **2 — Partially Meets** | Meets some but not all expectations; improvement needed | 0–3% increment; Performance Improvement Plan |
| **1 — Does Not Meet** | Consistently below expectations | No increment; formal PIP; potential separation |

### 6.7 Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

- Triggered when an employee receives a rating of 2 or below, or when performance concerns arise mid-cycle
- Duration: **60–90 days**
- Must include: specific improvement targets, support/resources, review meetings (bi-weekly), and consequences of non-improvement
- PIP is prepared by line manager, reviewed by HR Manager, and acknowledged by employee

### 6.8 Additional Elements (Controlled Version)

- **360-degree feedback:** For G4 (Senior Manager) and above; feedback from direct reports, peers, and supervisor
- **Calibration sessions:** HR and senior management review all ratings to ensure consistency and eliminate bias
- **Forced distribution guidance:** No more than 20% rated Outstanding; no more than 15% rated Does Not Meet
- **Succession planning linkage:** High performers (4–5 rating for 2+ years) entered into succession pipeline

## 7. Training and Development Policy

### 7.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited is committed to developing the skills and capabilities of its employees through structured training, education, and professional development programmes.

### 7.2 Scope

This policy applies to all confirmed employees. Probationary employees are eligible for mandatory and role-essential training only.

### 7.3 Training Categories

| Category | Description | Examples | Funding |
|----------|-------------|---------|---------|
| **Mandatory** | Required by law or policy | HSE, anti-harassment, data protection | 100% company funded |
| **Role-essential** | Required for current role performance | Software training, technical skills | 100% company funded |
| **Professional development** | Career growth and professional certifications | CPA, ACCA, PMP, SHRM | 50–100% company funded (per approval) |
| **Leadership development** | For current and future leaders (G3+) | Management courses, coaching | 100% company funded |
| **Personal development** | Employee-initiated, non-role-specific | Language courses, general skills | Employee funded; study leave may apply |

### 7.4 Training Needs Assessment

- Conducted annually as part of the performance review cycle
- Line managers identify skill gaps and recommend training
- HR consolidates into an annual Training Plan with budget
- Training Plan approved by MD (Lean) or HR Manager + CFO + MD (Controlled)

### 7.5 Training Budget

- **ASSUMPTION:** Annual training budget = **2% of total annual payroll costs**
- Budget allocated across departments based on headcount and strategic priorities
- Individual training requests above UGX 3,000,000 require MD approval
- Professional certification support capped at UGX 5,000,000 per employee per year

### 7.6 Training Bond

For training investments exceeding UGX 5,000,000 (cumulative per year), the employee must sign a training bond:

| Investment Level | Bond Duration |
|-----------------|---------------|
| UGX 5,000,000 – 10,000,000 | 12 months post-completion |
| UGX 10,000,001 – 20,000,000 | 18 months post-completion |
| Above UGX 20,000,000 | 24 months post-completion |

If the employee resigns before the bond period expires, they must reimburse the company on a pro-rata basis.

### 7.7 Training Evaluation

All training is evaluated using the Kirkpatrick model:

| Level | Evaluation | Method |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Level 1 — Reaction | Did the participant find the training useful? | Post-training feedback form |
| Level 2 — Learning | Did the participant acquire the intended knowledge/skills? | Test, assessment, or demonstration |
| Level 3 — Behaviour | Is the participant applying what they learned? | Manager observation at 30/60/90 days |
| Level 4 — Results | What is the business impact? | KPI improvement, error reduction |

- Annual Training Plan (prepared by HR)

## 8. Termination and Exit Policy

### 8.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited manages all employment separations in a fair, lawful, and dignified manner, in full compliance with the Employment Act, 2006 (Part IX — Termination of Employment).

### 8.2 Scope

This policy covers all forms of employment separation: resignation, dismissal, redundancy, retirement, expiry of contract, and death in service.

### 8.3 Types of Termination

| Type | Initiated By | Legal Basis |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Resignation** | Employee | Employment Act s.58 |
| **Dismissal (with cause)** | Employer | Employment Act s.65–69 |
| **Summary dismissal (gross misconduct)** | Employer | Employment Act s.69 |
| **Redundancy** | Employer | Employment Act s.81–82 |
| **Expiry of contract** | Automatic | Employment Act s.59 |
| **Retirement** | Policy/agreement | Company policy or contract |
| **Death in service** | N/A | Workers Compensation Act |
| **Mutual agreement** | Both parties | Employment Act s.58 |

### 8.4 Notice Periods

Per Employment Act s.58 and company policy (whichever is longer):

| Grade | Minimum Notice (Statute) | Company Notice Period |
|-------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| G1 — Junior | 2 weeks | 1 month |
| G2 — Officer | 2 weeks | 1 month |
| G3 — Manager | 1 month | 2 months |
| G4 — Senior Manager | 1 month | 3 months |
| G5 — Director | 1 month | 3 months |
| During probation | 2 weeks | 2 weeks |

Either party may pay salary in lieu of notice.

### 8.5 Redundancy

Per Employment Act s.81–82:

1. Employer must demonstrate genuine redundancy (economic reasons, reorganisation, technological change)
2. Employer must consult with affected employees (and union, if applicable) at least **1 month** before redundancy takes effect
3. Selection criteria must be fair and objective (LIFO — Last In First Out — unless skills-based selection is justified)
4. Severance pay: minimum **1 month's pay for each completed year of service**
5. Employee must be given priority for re-engagement within 12 months if the position is re-established

### 8.6 Final Pay Entitlements

Upon termination (any type, except summary dismissal for dishonesty/fraud), the employee is entitled to:

| Entitlement | Calculation |
|-------------|-------------|
| Salary to last working day | Pro-rated |
| Accrued unused annual leave | Days remaining x daily rate |
| Notice pay (if applicable) | Per notice period, if not served |
| Severance pay (redundancy only) | 1 month per completed year |
| Gratuity (if contractual) | Per contract terms |
| Repatriation (if applicable) | Per Employment Act s.38 |

Final pay must be processed within **7 working days** of the last working day.

### 8.7 Exit Process

| Step | Action | Responsible | Timeline |
|------|--------|------------|----------|
| 1 | Receive/issue termination notice | Employee/HR | As per notice period |
| 2 | Acknowledge and confirm last working day | HR Manager | Within 2 working days |
| 3 | Knowledge transfer and handover | Departing employee + Line Manager | During notice period |
| 4 | Exit interview | HR Manager (or HR Officer) | Last week of employment |
| 5 | Return company assets (laptop, keys, ID, phone) | Employee + IT + Admin | Last working day |
| 6 | Revoke system access | IT | Last working day (close of business) |
| 7 | Process exit checklist | HR Officer | Last working day |
| 8 | Calculate and process final pay | Finance + HR | Within 7 working days |
| 9 | Issue certificate of service | HR Manager | Within 7 working days |
| 10 | File NSSF cessation notice | HR Officer | Within 30 days |
| 11 | Archive employee file | HR Officer | Within 14 days |

### 8.8 Certificate of Service

Per Employment Act s.62, every employee leaving the service is entitled to a Certificate of Service stating:
- Name of employer and employee
- Date of commencement and termination
- Nature of employment / job title
- Wage rate at termination

The certificate must **not** contain any statement detrimental to the employee's character or future employment prospects.

## 9. Anti-Harassment Policy

### 9.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited has zero tolerance for harassment of any form. This policy is established in compliance with the Employment Act, 2006 and the Employment (Sexual Harassment) Regulations, 2012.

### 9.2 Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, interns, visitors, and business partners in all company premises, at company events, during business travel, and in any work-related context (including electronic communications).

### 9.3 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Harassment** | Any unwelcome conduct that is offensive, intimidating, or humiliating, and which creates a hostile work environment |
| **Sexual harassment** | Any unwelcome sexual advance, request for sexual favour, or other conduct of a sexual nature that affects the recipient's employment or creates an intimidating environment (Employment (Sexual Harassment) Regulations, 2012, Regulation 3) |
| **Bullying** | Repeated, unreasonable behaviour directed toward an employee that creates a risk to health and safety |
| **Victimisation** | Adverse treatment of a person because they have made or supported a complaint of harassment |

### 9.4 Examples of Prohibited Conduct

- Unwelcome physical contact or gestures
- Sexual comments, jokes, or innuendo
- Display of offensive or sexually explicit materials
- Repeated unwanted social invitations
- Threats or intimidation
- Offensive comments about a person's gender, ethnicity, religion, disability, or appearance
- Cyberbullying or harassment via electronic means
- Quid pro quo demands (conditioning employment benefits on sexual favours)

### 9.5 Reporting

| Channel | Contact |
|---------|---------|
| Direct supervisor | First point of contact (if appropriate) |
| HR Manager | Primary reporting channel |
| Managing Director | If complaint involves the HR Manager |
| Anonymous reporting | Written submission to sealed complaints box / designated email |

Employees are **encouraged** to report harassment. Failure by managers to act on reported harassment is itself a disciplinary offence.

### 9.6 Investigation Process

1. All complaints are treated **confidentially** and investigated promptly
2. HR Manager (or appointed independent investigator) conducts the investigation
3. Both complainant and respondent are interviewed separately
4. Witnesses are interviewed as necessary
5. Investigation completed within **15 working days**
6. Findings and recommendations presented to MD
7. Appropriate disciplinary action taken (up to and including dismissal)
8. Complainant informed of outcome
9. Follow-up support provided to affected parties

### 9.7 Protection Against Retaliation

- No employee shall face retaliation for making a good-faith complaint of harassment
- Retaliation itself constitutes a disciplinary offence warranting dismissal
- If the complaint is found to be knowingly false and malicious, the complainant may face disciplinary action

### 9.8 Training

- All employees receive anti-harassment training during onboarding and annually
- Managers receive additional training on recognising and addressing harassment
- Training records maintained by HR

## 10. Dress Code Policy

### 10.1 Policy Statement

Awdeer Investments Limited maintains a professional image and expects employees to dress appropriately for the workplace, considering their role, the nature of their duties, and any client-facing requirements.

### 10.2 Scope

This policy applies to all employees during working hours and when representing the company at external events.

### 10.3 Dress Code Standards

| Category | Standard | Examples |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Business formal** | Required for client-facing meetings, board meetings, and official functions | Suits, ties, formal dresses, closed shoes |
| **Business casual** | Standard office dress code | Collared shirts, khakis/trousers, blouses, modest dresses, closed shoes |
| **Smart casual** | Permitted on designated casual days (e.g., Fridays) | Neat jeans (no rips), polo shirts, smart trainers |
| **Operational/field** | As required by role | Company-branded shirts, safety boots, PPE |

### 10.4 General Guidelines

1. Clothing must be clean, neat, and in good repair
2. Visible tattoos and body piercings (other than ear piercings) should be discreet
3. Strong perfumes or colognes should be avoided
4. Personal hygiene must be maintained
5. Company-issued uniforms or branded clothing must be worn where applicable
6. Religious and cultural dress is respected, provided it does not create safety hazards

### 10.5 Prohibited Items

- Flip-flops or open-toed sandals (in office)
- Shorts (unless operational/field role)
- Clothing with offensive graphics or language
- Excessively revealing clothing
- Beachwear

### 10.6 Non-Compliance

- First occurrence: Verbal reminder from line manager
- Repeat occurrence: Written warning
- Persistent non-compliance: Disciplinary action per HR-DIS-001

### 10.7 Exceptions

Employees requiring accommodation for religious, cultural, medical, or disability reasons should discuss this with HR. Reasonable accommodations will be made.
